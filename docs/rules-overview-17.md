# Rules Overview — v17

*Drafted 1 November 2026 (evening) from Mark's decisions that day. **v16's game, with a turn order for everything outside the two reveals, and meter that buys cards.** v11 to v16 stay available; v16 is unchanged in `rules-overview 16.md` and on its own playtest page.*

- ***Two decks per fighter, two simultaneous steps, then the end of the round*** *(both draws, grey, the token), as in v16 with grey each round.*
- ***An initiative token*** *passes every round. Its holder wins speed ties and goes first wherever an order is needed. The neutral and clash reveals stay simultaneous.*
- ***Meter buys cards.*** *Each fighter has* ***two specials*** *(1 meter each, yours for the rest of the game once taken) and a* ***Super*** *(2 meter, used once), face up on the table.* ***Read*** *takes one into your hand instead of drawing.*
- ***A shared neutral core:*** *every fighter's 10-card neutral deck holds Charge ×2 and Read ×2, plus six signature cards.*

**Status: playable on the v17 playtest page and sanity-simmed** (Sim Check below). **Open now:** Counter-only Supers coming out rarely, and whether a Super that one Charge can buy becomes routine (watch it at the table; the sim can't tell us).

**Decisions (Mark, 1 November):**

- **Three pips were too slow for a Super** when Charge is the only source and a fighter runs two: the third pip needed a reshuffle. The Super now costs **2**.
- **The same Charge and Read count for everyone.** Two dials (Charge copies and Super cost) did one job; the Super's cost is the one printed where players see it.
- **Specials are separate, unique cards with their own art,** not upgrades of staples and not two-ended cards.
- **Meter unlocks cards rather than paying each use.** A special stays in your deck after it's played; **the Super is used once,** then removed from the game.
- **Specials and the Super start face up on the table,** not in hand (three dead cards in an opening hand of six felt bad), and **you select them instead of drawing at random.**
- **Selecting happens on Read, a neutral card,** not on a separate "buy" card (it would clog a hand of three whenever you couldn't pay) and not as a public draw-step action.
- **Charge may put a special from your discard pile into your hand** instead of gaining meter.
- **A turn order for everything outside the reveals:** choices after a reveal, Confiscate payments and discarding down to the hand limit all reveal or choose something in the open. The neutral step stays simultaneous: acting in turn there would hand the second player a free answer to the first player's power-up.
- **Everything at the end of a round happens together** (late on 1 November): draw a clash card, refill your neutral hand, discard down, take grey, pass the token. (The neutral hand briefly refilled at the reveal; it was one more thing to remember for no gain.)

**Decisions (Mark, 2 November):**

- **The initiative holder wins speed ties** (settling v16's Open Question 5).
- **The Warden at HP 34.**
- **Read draws 1** (or takes from the table). **Confiscate stays as written** (nothing below 2 cards): squeezing the hand is the Warden's gameplan, and it shouldn't be diluted.
- **Charge gains 2 meter (two in the core), specials cost 1, the Super costs 2.** One Charge can buy the Super, and that's fine: it's used once, and taking it costs a Read (no card drawn) in the open.
- *Considered and dropped the same morning:* Charge at 1 meter with three Charges in a deck of 11 (simmed: Warden 44–48%, mirror unchanged); a way to retrieve any card from the discard pile (it's taken instead of meter, so it doesn't add meter; still an idea for card recovery).

## Setup

1. Each player picks a fighter. **Toss for the initiative token.**
2. Put your **specials and Super face up on the table**. Shuffle your **clash deck** (without the specials) and your **neutral deck**.
3. Draw an **opening clash hand of 6** and a **neutral hand of 3**. Meter 0, piles empty.

## The Round

### 1. Neutral step (simultaneous)

1. **Everyone places one neutral card face down.** A knocked-down or Dizzy player places their marker instead and keeps their neutral hand.
2. **Reveal together.** Effects resolve, **the initiative holder's first**. A card with a choice (Read, Charge) is chosen now, **the holder first**, after both cards are face up.
3. Played neutral cards stay face up in front of you until the end of the round; a card that stays in play stays out.

### 2. Clash (simultaneous)

1. **Everyone places one clash card face down** (you must, if you hold one you can play). A Dizzy player's marker fills their slot.
2. **Reveal.** A card of a Locked type is voided. **Different types:** the triangle decides. **Same type:** the faster card wins; **a speed tie goes to the initiative holder.** **Two Guards:** both clear their own pile.
3. **Follow-up or combo** (only the winner acts, so no order is needed): a Guard that beat a Pressure may follow up with a Counter; a Starter that won may combo, a Linker then an Ender.
4. **Confiscate** (the Warden): owed discards are paid now, the holder first.
5. **Check for KO.**

### 3. End of round

All of it together, so there's one step to remember:

1. **Discard the cards you used** to their piles (neutral cards to the neutral discard). **A played Super is removed from the game.**
2. **Draw 1 clash card and refill your neutral hand to 3.**
3. **Discard down to your clash hand limit** (8; 5 under the Warden's Shackles), **the holder first.**
4. **Everyone adds 1 grey to their own pile.** Last round's statuses end.
5. **Pass the initiative token.**

## The Initiative Token

- **It's a tiebreaker and an order, not a resource.** Nothing gains or spends it; it simply passes every round.
- **The holder wins speed ties** in the clash. (Follow-ups and combo cards land unopposed, so they never tie.)
- **The holder goes first** wherever players act in turn: choices on revealed cards, Confiscate payments, discarding down. Going first means the other player sees your choice; winning ties is the balance.
- **It never orders a reveal.** Neutral and clash cards are always placed face down by both players before either is turned.

## Meter, Specials and the Super

- **Meter runs 0–3.** Its only source is **Charge** (2 meter each; two in every neutral deck).
- **Meter buys cards from the table, through Read:**
  - **A special costs 1** (printed on the card). It goes into your hand and from then on is an ordinary clash card: free to play, discarded, reshuffled, there for the rest of the game.
  - **The Super costs 2.** It goes into your hand (it doesn't count towards hand size or the hand limit) and costs nothing more to play. **Once played, won or lost, it's removed from the game.**
- **Charge can bring a special back:** instead of gaining meter, put a special from your discard pile into your hand.
- **What's public:** what you take from the table, and when. Everyone knows your Super is in your hand until you use it.
- **The table shows each card's state:** on the table (with its cost), taken, in hand (the Super) or used.

## The Shared Neutral Core

Every fighter's neutral deck is **10 cards: Charge ×2, Read ×2, and six signature cards.**

| Card | Copies | Text |
|---|---|---|
| **Charge** | 2 | **On reveal:** gain 2 meter. Or put a special from your discard pile into your hand instead. |
| **Read** | 2 | **On reveal:** draw a clash card. Or take a special or your Super from the table instead, paying its meter. |

- **Identity lives in the six signature cards** (and the specials and Super), not in resource counts.
- **Every neutral card must be worth playing in most board states:** a card you can't use clogs a hand of three badly.

## Authoring Rules (changes from v16)

- **Every choice is made in a simultaneous step, by the card you place, or in initiative order.** Anything else happens automatically.
- **Specials:** attacks, one copy each, two per fighter, cost 1. Stronger than the fighter's staples (that's what the meter buys) but still inside the triangle: a special can be read and beaten. Speed, an icon or a status, not a new verb.
- **Supers:** cost 2, used once. A Super that's only a Counter will rarely find a window; give it an Ender or a reason to be risked in the clash.
- **A neutral card may refer to the order** (the holder first); still never to the other player's neutral card.
- *Unchanged:* type effects are fixed; every Convert chips; Recovers never deal damage; Dizzy only on Supers; Lock on slow cards, Enders or cards with a cost; about a third of the clash deck as Counters.

## Sim Check (1 November, 300 games a row, Hard against Hard)

*A sanity check, not a tuning pass. The partner looks one round ahead, so it undervalues anything permanent (specials) or worth saving for (the Super): read the Super numbers as a floor. These ran with the neutral hand refilling at the reveal; moving the refill to the end of the round changes nothing the partner weighs, so they stand.*

| | Benchmark mirror | Warden against the Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Win (side 0) | 49% | 46%, 49% (two seeds) |
| Median game | 12 rounds | 12–12.5 |
| Supers played, a game | 0.6 (0.3 each) | Warden 0.05, Benchmark 0.4 |
| Specials taken, a game (each side) | Rising Strike 0.7, Breaker 0.65 | Chain Whip 0.9, Iron Haul 0.7 |
| Supers taken from the table, a game (each side) | 0.4 | Chain Prison 0.2, Overdrive 0.6 |
| Specials fetched back by Charge, a game | 0.65 | about 0.7 |
| Combos | 27.5% of rounds, 7.8 HP each | — |
| Speed ties (all to the holder) | 8% of rounds | 1% |
| Clash hand after a round | 4.7 | Warden 4.9, **Benchmark 2.5** |

**Before the sim-led fixes:** with Charge at 1, Supers came out 0.03–0.09 times a game per side whether Charge lost its fetch option, the core had three Charges, or Read drew 2. The Warden at HP 38 won 66% (58% with Read at draw 2); at HP 34, 53%.

**Compared with v16:** games are two rounds shorter, combos are much more common (16% → 27% of rounds: Rising Strike wins Pressure duels, and ties now go to someone), and hands are smaller (Read draws 1).

## Sim Check, 2 November (Read, Confiscate floor, Charge)

*300 games a row, Hard against Hard, neutral refill at the end of the round. "Starved" = the Benchmark goes into the clash holding 2 cards or fewer.*

| Charge · Super · Read · floor | Mirror: hand at the clash (at the limit of 8) | Warden (HP 34) win | Benchmark against the Warden: hand at the clash, starved |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · 2 · draw 1 · 2 | 6.1 (12%) | 51% | 3.8, **21%** |
| 1 · 2 · draw 2 · 2 | 6.9 (**37%**) | 48% | 4.3, 14% |
| 1 · 2 · draw 1 · **3** | 6.1 (12%) | 47% (53% at HP 36) | 4.1, **9%** |
| 2 · 3 · draw 1 · 3 | 6.1 (12%) | 40% (48% at HP 36) | 4.1, 9% |
| 1 · 2 · draw 1 · 2, Charge ×3, decks of 11 (dropped) | 6.1 (12%) · mirror 52% | **48%, 44%** (two seeds) | 3.9, 19% |

- **The starving is a Warden problem, not a combo problem.** In mirrors, with Read at draw 1, hands average 6 at the clash and almost never fall to 2 or fewer: the clash draw replaces the card you play, and combos are a cost the attacker chooses.
- **Read at draw 2 is a blunt fix:** against the Warden it helps a little (starved 21% → 14%), but in mirrors hands sit at the limit of 8 more than a third of the time, so draws are wasted and discards forced.
- **A Confiscate floor of 3 aims at the cause:** starved clashes fall to 9%, mirrors are untouched, and the Warden stays at 47% on HP 34. It's one number in Settings (Confiscate floor) and on the Confiscate reminder.
- **Supers in sim can't settle Charge.** The partner values a Super in hand at about 3–4 meter, so it almost never buys one at cost 3 (and rarely at cost 2 with Charge 1). The Super's frequency here reflects its weights, not the design.
- **Charge 1 (Super 2) against Charge 2 (Super 3):** both need two Charges for the Super. At Charge 1, a cycle of the neutral deck yields 2 meter: the Super *or* both specials. Permanent specials will usually win that choice, so the Super risks becoming the purchase nobody makes. At Charge 2 with a 3-meter Super, a cycle yields 4: both specials early and the Super later, or one special and the Super. Meter overflows at 3, so holding meter has a cost. The Warden needs HP 36 under it (40% at 34, though ±6).
- **Outcome:** Mark kept the floor at 2 (the squeeze is the Warden's plan; 19–21% of the Benchmark's clashes against him come with 2 cards or fewer, by design) and, after briefly choosing Charge 1 with three Charges, went back to **Charge 2, Super 2**, as simmed on 1 November (Warden 46–49% at HP 34).

## Design Flags

1. **The Super may become routine:** one Charge and one Read buy it, so expect it in most games, held as a public threat. That's the intent (it's used once); if it starts deciding games, raise it to 3.
2. **Counter-only Supers are rare:** the Warden takes Chain Prison 0.2 times a game and plays it 0.05. Give it Ender, or let it be thrown out as a raw read.
3. **The Warden squeezes hard under Read draw 1:** his opponent ends rounds holding 2.5 clash cards. He's balanced at HP 34, but check at the table that it still feels like playing (Warden Flag 1). Shackles back to 2 copies is the first lever.
4. **Combos jumped** (27% of rounds). Watch whether the initiative's tie wins make rounds swingy in mirrors.
5. **The initiative token's value** isn't measured yet (holder win rate by round).
6. **Specials are taken about two-thirds of the time each per game** in sim; humans who plan ahead will likely take both earlier. Watch whether unlocking becomes automatic (then raise specials to 2).
7. **Kept from v16:** Guard spam, Rush's payoff, Parry and Breakfall, card counting.

## Open Questions

1. **Read is the bottleneck for buying:** two Reads a cycle, each a choice between a card and a purchase. Watch whether players sit on meter waiting for a Read.
2. **Special and Super costs per fighter** (1 and 2 here).
3. **Who starts with the initiative** (a toss here); whether it should pass to the loser of the clash instead of alternating.
4. **Names:** "special" for the meter cards; "the table" for where they wait.
5. *From v16:* combo length and breakers; reshuffling costs; Gems.

## Kept From v16

Split clash and neutral decks; the neutral step and its hand of 3; the clash, triangle, speed bands and combos; the Guard follow-up (Counters only); statuses; grey each round and HP about a quarter higher; "one card each, every time"; Lock voiding; every Convert chipping; type effects fixed; the Warden's Confiscate and Shackles.

## Removed

**Compared with v16:** the Super held in hand from the start and paid for when played; reusable Supers; speed ties cancelling (they go to the initiative holder); "no card text may depend on resolution order" (replaced by the initiative order); fighters choosing their own Charge and Read counts.
